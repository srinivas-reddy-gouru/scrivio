# Watching ten people use it

**No sessions have been held.** This is how to hold them.

The purpose is to find where people stop. It is not to collect praise, and
nothing said in a session is to be quoted publicly without that person's
written agreement.

## Who

Ten people who are applying for jobs now or did in the last six months. Not
colleagues on this project. Aim for a spread: some who write code and some
who do not, some early in their careers, at least two who are not native
English speakers, at least two who have never run a program from a terminal.

If ten cannot be found, hold as many as can and report that number. Do not
describe five sessions as ten.

## Their data

- They choose whether to use their own resume or the sample. Either is
  useful. Do not press.
- If they use their own, it stays on their machine. Do not ask them to share
  their screen while their resume is on it unless they offer. Do not record
  it.
- Tell them before they start that their resume will be sent to the model
  provider that is configured, and which one that is.
- Keep notes by participant number, not by name.
- Afterwards, show them **Settings, Your data, Delete everything**.

## The session, forty minutes

**Setup (not counted in the time).** Decide beforehand whether you are
testing the install. If you are, give them the README and nothing else. If
you are not, have it running.

| | Ask them to | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Look at the home page for thirty seconds, then say what this is for and who it is for. | Whether they can. What they think it does that it does not. |
| 2 | Check a resume against a posting. | Where they hesitate. Whether they find the sample. Whether they read the note about where the resume is sent. |
| 3 | Say what the score means. | Whether they take it as a prediction of getting an interview. |
| 4 | Tailor it, and get a copy they could send. | What they do at `[METRIC]`. Whether they understand why the download is refused. Whether they read the rewrite or trust it. |
| 5 | Find one thing in the rewrite they would change, and change it. | Whether they can. |
| 6 | Practise one interview question. | Voice or typing. What they make of the feedback. |
| 7 | Say whether they would use it again, and for what. | The reason, more than the answer. |

Say as little as possible. When they are stuck, wait. If they ask what to do,
ask what they expected. Help only when they would otherwise give up, and
write down that you did.

## Write down, for each person

- How far they got without help: steps 1 to 7.
- Every place they stopped for more than ten seconds.
- Every place you helped.
- What they said the score meant.
- Whether they noticed the rewrite had changed something they did not want.
- Whether they got a copy they would send.
- Own resume or sample.

## Afterwards

Count, do not summarise. "Six of ten did not find the send step" is a
finding. "People liked it" is not.

A week later, ask each person once whether they opened it again. Record yes,
no, or no reply. No reply is not a no.

## What this cannot tell you

Ten people is enough to find the places where most people stop. It is not
enough to say what proportion of all users would, or whether the tailoring is
good, or whether anyone got an interview because of it.
